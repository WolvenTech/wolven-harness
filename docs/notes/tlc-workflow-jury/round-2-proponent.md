# Round 2 — Proponent

**Hold: B for the bounded feasibility trial, with R independently selected. Confidence: medium.** This remains B as proposed, not permanent adoption and not permission to implement. I accept the majority's cost concern without treating its vote count as evidence of effectiveness.

The strongest opposing argument is the Skeptic's concrete objection in `round-1-record.md`: identifying every relevant command input is the central unresolved safety problem, and a digest over an incomplete set can look more authoritative than it deserves. Risk adds the complementary operational failure: overbroad exclusions produce false freshness, while excessive invalidation creates pressure to override the gate. Integrator correctly observes that B requires building its integration before the single-feature feasibility trial; the trial is not a cheap way to avoid that engineering investment. These arguments materially limit the case for B. Four proposed scripts, not five, still constitute a distributed executable contract with real maintenance costs.

I nevertheless retain B because the record also makes the residual C risk unusually explicit. Skeptic identifies untriggered CLI semantics, validation and UI behavior; Integrator says R cannot substitute because review remains ask-only; Evidence proposes deliberately weak assertions and stale evidence as falsification controls. These are not obscure hazards outside the decision frame. C's principal enforcement remains the author's inspection and bookkeeping, including classification of whether independence is required. B attempts two separable protections: executable rejection of mechanically invalid evidence and a fresh assessment of assertion adequacy for every non-tiny feature. That is the assurance level I would prefer to evaluate before settling permanently for manual enforcement.

The facts are the existing verifier refusal, preserved-file installer behavior, unimplemented B machinery, and absence of comparative outcome evidence. My preference for paying a bounded engineering cost to test broader assurance is a value judgment. The majority's preference to introduce less machinery first is also a defensible value judgment; neither is an empirical finding that one option produces better delivery.

The observability requirement should remain severe: on the already-wanted two-unit feature, predeclare the implementation/verification budget and evaluation owner, then reject deliberate empty-selection, stale relevant untracked-input and green weak-assertion controls before closure. Record gate-maintenance effort and false allegations as costs. A false PASS or excess cost without useful evidence defeats my reason for retaining B; success establishes feasibility only.

Before any implementation, resolve the exact identity input set, command/count portability, red-baseline disposition, Human acceptance of degraded assurance and workflow grandfathering. Evidence's high-risk one-file shortcut conflict must be corrected consistently across spec and execution. The shared brief's absent `site/guide/` target and A+R preparation dependency also need correction in the later specification. R remains worthwhile independently: visible unavailable checks, stable findings and refreshed head grounding offer narrower benefits without B's runtime. None of these recommendations supplies user approval or remote-action authority.
