# Integrator — round 2

**Choice:** Hold B — narrow authentic behavioral evidence with controls and evidence-led amendments. Endorse the amended direction, subject to campaign-accounting clarification before the executable gate is locked.

**Rationale:** The decision rubric remains evidence of current canon retrieval/application, resistance to false passes, feasibility and preservation of consumer contracts. The amended R1/R2/R4 and C2 now address my central round-1 objections: complete observable actions rather than QMD events alone, executor qualification, independent authoritative source comparison, and a stale-index transition. R7/R9 enumerate critical violations and reject aggregate-rate waivers. The draft ADR appropriately narrows its inference to observable retrieval and answer consistency, and the deferral now excludes minimum freshness proof. These are material improvements to B, rather than reasons to choose A or C. There is still no authentic live proof or verified executor, so endorsement is of the method, not a finding of compliance.

**Concrete shared arguments affecting my judgment:** No argument changed my option. The proponent's round-1 amendment 4 supplies a useful scope reduction: a passing baseline should not trigger a duplicate candidate campaign. The skeptic's amendment 5 supplies a counterweight to overfitting: diagnose on paired runs and confirm a changed candidate on held-out seeds. I support both additions now present in R9, provided their actual run cost is stated accurately. The evidence seat's amendment 4 supports the amended R4/ADR distinction between observable answer consistency and private causal reasoning. The risk seat's amendment 3 supports requiring conclusion/write ordering in the executor qualification, now present in R1/R2. These arguments sharpen the rubric without overturning my original choice.

**Agreements:** All five seats agree that A cannot demonstrate execution, that mandatory calls in C would not by themselves establish correct applicability/use, and that B requires authentic evidence and independent controls. All identify stale indexes as a gap in the original current-canon claim. This is agreement on factual coverage and the interpretation of the promised benefit, not evidence that the proposed executor or evaluator already works. All support conditional enforcement escalation and bounded claims.

**Disagreements and unresolved tradeoffs:** There is no substantive option-level dissent in the shared record. The seats differ in confidence and emphasis. The risk seat's freshness proposal centers an authorized refreshed second run, while other seats explicitly require read-only stale-index blocking as well; the amended C2 appropriately preserves both. The proponent's baseline-pass shortcut favors cost control, while the skeptic's held-out confirmation favors stronger resistance to prompt tuning. Those can coexist through a conditional campaign. Shared unanimity should not hide unresolved feasibility, the cost added by separately scored variants, or the absence of ordinary-consumer evidence.

**Required clarification before locking R9:** R9 still states 60 baseline runs and 60 paired plus 60 held-out candidate runs, but C2 separately requires ten seeds for valid-chain, conflict, stale read-only and stale maintenance-authorized outcomes. With C1/C3/C4/C5/C6 receiving ten each, that is 90 baseline executions and, if a candidate is needed, 90 paired plus 90 held-out candidate executions: 270 total. If a different packing is intended, specify exactly how separately scored variants map to sessions and denominators. Do not retain the 60-run claim while silently increasing the campaign. This arithmetic matters to the smallest-credible-method criterion and U-budget.

**Residual risks and recommendations:**

1. U-executor remains the principal feasibility gate. Demonstrate complete event capture, actual instruction loading, oracle isolation and cleanup with the proposed qualification artifact before substantial runner work. A nominal trace sequence cannot prove capture completeness.
2. Define the freshness comparator at matching scope: returned decisive ranges must be compared with corresponding authoritative ranges, while status/supersession/applicability evidence must also be retrieved. A partial response hash should not be compared directly with an entire-file hash. Pin the authoritative goal-start snapshot or detect source changes during a run; otherwise later maintenance could make the snapshot obsolete.
3. The evaluator-owned manifest cannot be the agent's freshness diagnostic mechanism because it is outside agent access. C2 should use ordinary permitted source reads or a verified runtime freshness signal, captured in the trace, without exposing expected answers or requiring a QMD directive in the natural goal. An evaluator-detected mismatch is not evidence the agent recognized it.
4. Existing collection-trust and maintenance-authorization contradictions still require evidence-led diagnosis under R8. The amended design describes the correct policy but does not yet demonstrate that installed consumer instructions induce it.
5. A passing synthetic campaign remains bounded to tested runtime/model/instruction bytes and goals. It does not establish universal consumer reliability or enforcement. Retain the persistent-critical-bypass and real-incident escalation triggers; do not replace prevention needs with favorable benchmark averages.

**Main risk if wrong:** The refined campaign could pass while normal consumer sessions still apply stale canon or bypass retrieval, creating unjustified trust in the project's principal upside. Alternatively, executor and campaign scope could grow enough that B becomes an evaluation platform before delivering its first authentic proof.

**Own confidence:** Medium overall; high that B is preferable to A/C now, medium that the amended method is executable and sufficient for its bounded claims until qualification and scorer calibration are demonstrated.
